# Research + gap analysis + suggestions: in-match tactics (all formats), the auction, national selection, franchise/international/domestic integration, coaching earnings, partnerships, media

Written after the follow-up pass (584/584). This is a **planning / decision document**, not an
implementation. It pairs web research on how real cricket actually works with a gap analysis
against what CLAUDE.md says we've built, then a large set of suggestions, each with reasoning,
where it plugs in, and a rough effort tier (S = a slice, M = 2-3 slices, L = a phase-sized piece).

Sources are listed at the very end.

---

## PART A — RESEARCH FINDINGS (what real cricket does)

### A1. In-match tactical depth — T20

- **Three phases, three different games.** Powerplay (1-6): 2 out, attack the new ball, but the
  new ball also swings for 2-3 overs so the bowling side has a genuine wicket window. Middle
  (7-15/16): the field spreads (5 out), boundaries dry up, it becomes a **dot-ball pressure /
  smart-accumulation** contest — spin and cutters, building pressure through dots rather than
  chasing wickets. Death (16/17-20): yorkers, wide slower balls, hitting the deep-set field.
- **Matchups drive the batting order, not just the bowling.** Teams **promote a left-hander when
  leg-spin is on** to force a line/field change and break the bowler's rhythm; hold a player back
  for a specific matchup; send a pinch-hitter when quick runs are needed.
- **Field placement follows the specific bowler-batter matchup** — a deep cover for a batter who
  lofts over mid-off; a packed off-side ring against a bottom-hand player; a boundary rider on the
  batter's strongest scoring arc.
- **Impact Player rule** (IPL/BBL, retained to 2027) — a 12th player named pre-match, substituted
  in during the game. Lifted overall scoring, arguably at the cost of all-rounder development.
- **Analytics are everywhere** — pre-match study of the opposition's strike/economy splits by
  phase, by bowling type, ground dimensions, scoring trends; RCB/KKR/RR run 10-30-strong
  analyst/scout teams.

### A2. In-match tactical depth — ODI

- **Three field-restriction blocks:** 1-10 (2 out), 11-40 (4 out), 41-50 (5 out — no separate
  batting powerplay any more; the last 10 are just 5 out).
- **Middle overs are the underrated phase** — the fielding captain rotates bowlers to build
  pressure with dots and part-time spin, the batting side "consolidates" (an anchor + a rotator),
  and the phase "dictates terms" for the death.
- **Two new balls** (one per end) means the ball never ages enough to reverse or grip — which is
  itself a tactical fact (spinners bowl in the middle with a hard ball; reverse swing is dead in
  ODIs). Sachin has publicly argued to scrap the rule to bring reverse back.
- **Batting depth** — a side batting to No. 8 attacks earlier than one batting to No. 6.

### A3. In-match tactical depth — Test

- **Declaration is a live decision at any ball**, weighed against: lead, overs left, weather
  forecast (declare before rain wipes the match), pitch wear, the opposition's batting quality.
  Bazball: Stokes declared at 592/4 to buy time to bowl Australia out — sacrificing runs for overs.
- **Follow-on** (200-run lead in a 5-day Test) — demoralising, but modern captains enforce it less
  because of a tiring attack and the prospect of batting last on a worn pitch.
- **Session-by-session strategic intent** — a side plays for a win, a draw, or "don't lose",
  and reassesses at every interval; momentum matters, one bad session doesn't.
- **Bad light / floodlights** — umpires can offer the light; a side pressing for a win wants every
  over, a side under pressure is happy to go off.
- **Nightwatchman** — a tail-ender promoted near the close to protect a specialist in poor light.
- **Field settings swing hard between attacking (slips, short leg, silly point) and defensive
  (sweepers, deep-set) based on match situation, not just the batter.**

### A4. Bowling & batting PLANS by format

- **New-ball plan** (all formats) — a repeatable stock ball (good length, 4th/off stump) first,
  then the wicket ball as a weapon; set a field for the in-swinger, keep the out-swinger as the
  surprise. A sharp bowler **changes the plan mid-spell on feel/rhythm**.
- **Reverse-swing plan** (Test, and old-ball ODIs pre-2011) — start outside off to make the batter
  commit, then the ball tails back into pad/stumps: bowled and LBW are the classic reverse
  dismissals. Needs the ball genuinely old (25-45 overs).
- **Spin plans** — Test: bowl into the rough outside a left-hander's leg stump, invite the drive,
  top-spin for LBW, the arm ball; ODI/T20: containment lines outside off, into the pitch, use the
  crease width.
- **A plan only works against a batter it troubles AND a bowler who can bowl it** — this is the
  point of an analyst report: bouncing a poor puller is a plan, bouncing a good one is a gift.

### A5. Partnership planning — batting AND bowling

- **Batting:** calling conventions need rehearsal (striker calls in front, non-striker behind);
  the best pairs never refuse a sensible single, never run a bad one; **complementary styles**
  (Trescothick's aggression + Atherton's defence) — one anchors, one rotates/attacks; both scan
  the field every ball for the single. Poor communication = run-outs that cancel the benefit.
- **Bowling:** new-ball pairs feed off each other (one attacks the stumps, one the corridor;
  pressure from one end forces a mistake at the other); a captain rotates a quick in short bursts
  and brings him back rather than bowling him out.

### A6. The auction — squad building, identification, strategy, bidding wars

- **Months of prep.** Coaches + analysts + scouts review last season, identify the roles to
  strengthen (power hitter, death bowler, spin specialist, keeper), and build a **blueprint** —
  the goal is a *balanced, role-covered squad*, not a collection of stars.
- **Identification:** watch across formats for T20 adaptability; study strike/economy/impact/
  pressure metrics; a **year-round scouting laboratory** (MI "as many as needed", KKR 25-30).
- **The single most overvalued commodity is name recognition** — big social-media names sell
  30-50% above statistical value. The **Moneyball** franchises (CSK, MI, RR) run a "silent
  strategy": avoid bidding wars (winning one usually means overpaying and unbalancing the squad),
  stay patient, wait for value, target the accelerated round.
- **Structure:** marquee sets (2 × ~6, mega only) → capped by role → uncapped by role →
  **accelerated round** (after the first ~70-116, franchises submit a shortlist of who comes next;
  then a final segment for the still-unsold). By the accelerated phase, **squad limits, the
  overseas quota and remaining purse dictate who gets picked**, not merit.
- **Budget discipline:** overspend early → no options late. Retain 5 capped → only ₹45cr of ₹120cr
  left.
- **RTM:** original club matches the winning bid → the winning bidder gets **one final raise** →
  original club decides at the new price (RR kept Jaiswal this way).
- **Retention 2025:** up to 6 (max 5 capped, max 2 uncapped); RTM back for a mega auction; Impact
  Player to 2027; a per-match fee (₹7.5 lakh) introduced.
- **Overseas:** max 8 in a 18-25 squad, max 4 in the XI. Domestic-overseas balance is a **major
  auction axis**.

### A7. National team management — selection, XI, rotation

- **A selection panel** (chairman of selectors + ex-players, board-appointed) picks squad, series,
  tour. In Australia the **captain sits on the panel**; pre-series consultation where the captain
  advocates for emerging talent or a plan against a weakness.
- **Selection factors:** current form, domestic-league form, fitness, performance under pressure,
  and **workload management in constant contact with the medical team**.
- **Three formats, one year** — balance the workload across all three, plan player development,
  often **separate captains** per format.
- **Central contracts** now explicitly acknowledge franchise income — PCB tiers players by how
  many overseas leagues they may play (track AB: 1, BC: 2, C: uncapped); ECB overhauled its
  central-contract system in response to the franchise circuit.

### A8. Franchise ↔ international ↔ domestic integration

- **NOC (No Objection Certificate)** — a centrally contracted player needs his board's NOC to
  play an overseas league; the board can and does **deny** it (PCB denied Usama Mir the T20
  Blast). All NOCs are subject to national duty.
- **Scheduling clashes are the defining tension** — franchise seasons increasingly overlap
  bilateral series; **England's 2024 SA tour was scrapped because it clashed with SA20**.
- **The freelance-coach circuit** — a small number of coaches run multiple franchise teams across
  leagues (Voges at Perth = 65 BBL games / 3 titles; McCullum KKR then England; Ponting 96 T20
  franchise matches + IPL titles; Flower/Moody across several). **Hybrid income** (a board contract
  + franchise leagues) can exceed **$2m/year**. Director-of-Cricket sits above the head coach and
  owns the auction plan + coaching structure + pathway.

### A9. Media & news — worldwide, per team, per competition, per auction

- **Play-by-play + expert analysis**, split across many broadcasters as rights fragment; each
  builds its own commentary team. Ex-player pundits are **conflicted** — "obliged to boost
  struggling incumbents", praise their boards "for fear of losing their jobs", shout catchphrases.
- **Press accreditation / mixed zone / press-conference room** — a formal media-access structure.
- **National press rivalries** (the Australian vs Indian cricket press view each other with
  suspicion — a real cultural dynamic).
- **Awards ceremonies** — Orange/Purple Cap, MVP, Emerging Player (₹10 lakh), Fairplay, Most
  Sixes/Fours, and a **narrative** the media builds across a tournament (breakout star, flop,
  comeback, captaincy under fire).

## PART C — SUGGESTIONS (with reasoning)

Grouped. Each: **Why** (the realism/innovation case) · **What it adds** · **Where it plugs in** ·
**Effort**.

### C1 — In-match tactical depth, ALL formats

**1. The AI reads its own analyst dossier in-match.** `AnalystService.PrepareReport` /
`ApplyToPlan` already produce `TacticalPlan.MatchupApproaches` and it's tested — but nothing calls
it in a live `FixturePlayService` match. *Why:* it's the single biggest "built but unwired" gap,
and it's what makes a good analyst worth paying for. *What:* `FixturePlayService` builds each
side's analyst report from its hired analyst + `AnalysisQualityBoost`, applies it to the
`TacticalPlan` it passes into the match; a weak analyst's report is confidently wrong and costs
the side (already modelled). *Where:* `FixturePlayService.PlayLimitedOvers/PlayMultiDay` → build a
`TacticalPlan` where today it passes null. *Effort:* **M** (thread a plan through; calibrate).

**2. Situation-driven field aggression, not just batter-driven.** Real captains swing
attacking↔defensive on the *match state* (chasing a target, defending a low score, last hour of a
Test pressing for a win). We have `FieldAggression` per phase in `TacticalPlan` and
`MatchAmbitionService` for Tests — connect them. *Why:* a defensive field in the last over of a
chase leaks the winning runs; an attacking field when 300 ahead with a day left is throwing it
away — both are real captaincy errors we should let happen and let good captains avoid. *What:*
`AutoFieldSetter` takes a `situationAggression` derived from the chase equation / `MatchAmbition`;
scaled by captaincy competence (a poor captain misreads it). *Where:* `AutoFieldSetter.BuildField`
+ `InningsSimulator` per-over. *Effort:* **S-M**.

**3. Left-hander promotion vs spin (auto).** The classic T20/ODI move: send a left-hander in when
leg-spin comes on. We have `InMatchTacticalAI.ChoosePinchHitter` — add a sibling. *Why:* it's one
of the most visible tactical levers in the modern game and we model everything around it
(`ApplySpinDirection` already knows left vs right matters) but never actually make the call.
*What:* when a wicket falls and a wrist-spinner is bowling, a captain with the judgement for it
promotes the best-available left-hander a place or two, gated by `DecisionAuthority` and captaincy
quality. *Where:* `InningsSimulator.TryBringInNextBatter`. *Effort:* **S**.

**4. Reverse swing as a real plan.** Once the ball is genuinely old (>~35 overs, first-class /
old-rules ODI only), a bowler with the skill can bowl a reverse-swing plan: start wide, tail it
back, dismissal mix shifts hard to **bowled + LBW**. *Why:* it's a defining Test skill, it's a
real reason the follow-on and the third-innings collapse happen, and we have every hook
(`DeliveryEffectService.DismissalWeightsFor`, ball age, `Bowling.Swing`). Two-new-ball ODIs
correctly get *no* reverse — which itself teaches the player why that rule is controversial.
*What:* a `BowlingApproach.ReverseSwing` preset + a `DeliveryEffectService` branch gated on ball
age + `Bowling.Swing`/`Seam` + a dry, abrasive surface. *Where:* `DeliveryEffectService`,
`PlanFitService.CandidatePlans`. *Effort:* **S-M**.

**5. Middle-overs "consolidation vs pressure" as an explicit contest (ODI).** Model the ODI middle
(11-40) as its own phase where the batting side picks *anchor + rotator* and the bowling side
picks *contain (dots) vs attack (wickets)*, and the winner of that contest sets up the death.
*Why:* research calls it "the most underrated phase" and "dictates terms for the death"; we
collapse it into a generic phase. *What:* extend `BattingIntent` with an `Accumulate` mode that
farms singles and protects wickets; `MatchAmbitionService`-style middle-overs read for the
fielding captain (are we ahead of par → attack; behind → contain). *Where:* `InningsSimulator`
phase handling. *Effort:* **M**.

**6. Impact Player as a real substitution.** Currently a flat ±2.5%. Model it properly: name a
12th player pre-match; the batting side can sub him in for a non-batter when a wicket falls in the
powerplay/early middle (extra batting depth), or the bowling side subs a bowler for a batter after
the 6th over. *Why:* it genuinely changed IPL tactics (teams bat deeper, all-rounders devalued —
Rohit's public complaint) and we should model that trade-off, not paper over it. *What:*
`XiSelectionResult.ImpactPlayer`; `InningsSimulator` allows one substitution per side per innings
at defined trigger points; the sub joins the batting order / bowling rotation. *Where:*
`XiSelectionService`, `InningsSimulator`, `MatchSetup`. *Effort:* **M-L** (the innings sim has no
substitution concept today — flagged as deferred in CLAUDE.md).

**7. Declaration as a captain-quality call, not a formula.** `InningsSimulator.ShouldDeclare` is
"~3.2/over + a cushion, ≥55 overs left". Make it read the captain's `TacticalJudgement`, the
**weather forecast** (declare early if rain is coming — Bazball's whole point), the pitch wear,
and the opposition's batting quality; a bold captain (Stokes) declares 40 runs "early", a cautious
one bats too long and gifts the draw. *Why:* declaration timing is *the* Test captaincy skill and
we make it deterministic. *Where:* `MultiDayMatchSimulator` + `CaptaincyService`. *Effort:* **S-M**.

**8. Bowling-pair pressure transfer.** Extend `BowlingPairSynergyService`: dots/pressure built at
one end raise the wicket probability at the *other* end (the batter takes a risk against the
"easier" bowler to release pressure). *Why:* it's how new-ball pairs actually work and how spin
pairs strangle a middle order; we have the pair-identity plumbing, just not the effect. *Where:*
`BallOutcomeModel` reads a `PartnerPressure` term from `BowlerMatchState`. *Effort:* **S**.

**9. Batting partnership roles (anchor / rotator / aggressor) with a fit bonus.** A pair of
*complementary* styles (one anchors, one rotates) outperforms two of the same. *Why:* research is
explicit (Trescothick+Atherton); we track chemistry (history) but not *style fit* (a-priori).
*What:* derive a partnership-role from `BattingTraits`; a complementary pair gets a small
run-rate/stability bonus, two blockers or two sloggers a penalty. *Where:*
`PartnershipChemistryService` / a new small read in `InningsSimulator`. *Effort:* **S**.

**10. A `Communication` / `Running` attribute driving the run-out floor.** Research: "calling
conventions need rehearsal… poor communication = run-outs that cancel the benefit". We have a
run-out risk multiplier from chemistry but no *player attribute* for it. *Why:* it explains why
some individually brilliant batters are terrible runners, and it's a real scouting axis. *What:*
add `Mental.RunningCalling` (or reuse `GameAwareness` + a new small field); it sets a floor under
run-out risk that chemistry can't fully mitigate. *Where:* `PlayerAttributes`, `BallOutcomeModel`
run-out branch. *Effort:* **S** (but touches the player model — do it deliberately).

### C2 — The auction: variety, identity, and other franchises reacting

**11. Franchise auction ARCHETYPES.** Give each franchise a persistent auction identity:
*Moneyball* (CSK/MI/RR — avoid bidding wars, target value + the accelerated round, heavy scout
weighting), *Star-hunter* (RCB — overpay for marquee names, name-recognition bias), *Balanced*,
*Youth-builder* (heavy reserve-list + uncapped focus). *Why:* right now every franchise brain is
the same `BuildPlan`, so auctions feel identical run to run; archetypes are the single biggest
realism win here and they're cheap. *What:* a `FranchiseAuctionIdentity` enum on `Team` (or
derived from `Board.Ambition` + `CulturalIdentity` + scout quality) that reshapes `BuildPlan`'s
tiers/ceilings and the purse-pressure response. *Where:* `FranchiseAuctionService.BuildPlan` /
`Ceiling`. *Effort:* **M**.

**12. Name-recognition overvaluation as a real bias.** Research: the most overvalued commodity is
name recognition; big names sell 30-50% above statistical value; the Moneyball teams exploit this.
*Why:* it's *the* auction inefficiency and it lets a smart franchise (or the human) genuinely
out-trade the AI. *What:* `InterestVote` / `Ceiling` gets a `reputation` inflation term scaled
UP for a Star-hunter archetype and DOWN (toward statistical value) by the franchise's **scouting
department quality** (`ChiefScout` + analyst count). *Where:* `FranchiseAuctionService`. *Effort:* **S-M**.

**13. Live transparency the other franchises actually use.** We produce `AuctionSummary` (a
read-model) but the bidding logic doesn't react to it mid-auction. *Why:* real franchises watch
who's spent up and adjust — "RCB have blown their purse, we can relax on the next quick". *What:*
`Ceiling` already has a purse-pressure term reading rivals' remaining purse; extend it to react to
*role saturation* (rivals who've filled this role drop out → I don't need to escalate) and to a
rival's *plan progress* (a rival whose must-haves are done is dangerous on an unplanned name).
*Where:* `FranchiseAuctionService.Ceiling`. *Effort:* **S** (mostly there).

**14. A Director of Cricket layer.** Above the head coach: owns the auction plan, the coaching
structure, and the pathway; a strong DoC (Sangakkara at RR, KP-as-mentor at DC) measurably
improves auction outcomes and squad balance over multiple years. *Why:* it's a real, distinct
role that the franchise world runs on and we don't model it at all; it's also the natural home
for the archetype (C11). *What:* a `StaffRole.DirectorOfCricket`; when present, `BuildPlan`
accuracy and the retention decision improve, and the DoC (not the campaign coach) carries the
franchise's multi-year identity. *Where:* `SpecialistStaffService`, `FranchiseAuctionService`,
`FranchiseRetentionService`. *Effort:* **M**.

**15. Retention as a negotiation, not a formula.** Research: retention is "rethinking loyalty" —
a player held at a slab well below market can agitate (we have this), but there's also a real
*negotiation* (the franchise offers a slab, the player counters, they settle or he enters the
auction). *Why:* it makes the mega-auction build-up a story and gives the player agency. *What:*
`FranchiseRetentionService` gains a 2-3 round offer/counter driven by the player's
`MoneyFocused`/`Loyal` and the gap between slab and market. *Where:* `FranchiseRetentionService`.
*Effort:* **S-M**.

**16. Scouting-department SIZE changes plan quality.** MI "as many as needed", KKR 25-30, RR 10
analysts. *Why:* it's a real, visible differentiator and it gives the board a meaningful thing to
spend on. *What:* `Team.ScoutingDepartmentSize` (or count of `Scout`/`ChiefScout`/`DataAnalyst`
staff) reduces the noise in `InterestVote` and the name-recognition bias, and widens `BuildPlan`'s
covered pool. *Where:* `FranchiseAuctionService`, `SpecialistStaffService`. *Effort:* **S**.

**17. The accelerated round as a genuine value phase.** We have an accelerated round but it's
"unsold, best-first". Research: franchises *submit a shortlist* of who comes next, and by then
squad limits + quota + purse dictate picks — bargains happen here. *Why:* the Moneyball archetype
should *target* this phase; the human should be able to. *What:* each franchise nominates its
accelerated-round shortlist from its plan's un-filled roles; a franchise that saved purse gets
first pick of a genuine bargain. *Where:* `FranchiseAuctionService` accelerated block. *Effort:* **S-M**.

### C3 — National selection, central contracts, NOC

**18. Central-contract tiers that gate franchise participation.** PCB: track AB → 1 overseas
league, BC → 2, C → uncapped. ECB overhauled its whole system for this. *Why:* it's the defining
modern board-vs-franchise tension and we model neither side of it. *What:* `PlayerContract` (or a
new `CentralContract` on the national `Team`) with a tier that limits how many
`ContractKind.Franchise` deals a contracted international may hold in a year; a top-tier player is
*paid more* to compensate. *Where:* `NationalSelectionService`, `FranchiseAuctionService`
(eligibility), `SeasonFinanceService`. *Effort:* **M**.

**19. NOC grant/deny as a board decision.** A centrally contracted player needs his board's NOC to
play an overseas league; the board denies it when there's a national clash or to make a point
(PCB/Usama Mir). *Why:* it's the mechanism behind the whole integration story and it's a great
source of player-vs-board drama. *What:* before the franchise auction / before a franchise
signing, a contracted international's board rolls an NOC decision (yes / yes-with-conditions /
no) based on the fixture clash, the player's importance, and board–player relationship; a denial
pulls him from the auction pool. *Where:* `FranchiseAuctionService` pool build, a new
`NationalBoardService.ConsiderNoc`. *Effort:* **M**.

**20. A selection-panel meeting with a rationale + dissent record.** We have `SelectionPanelService`
(distortion) — add the *meeting*: the panel + captain produce a short rationale per pick and a
dissent note when the chairman overrules. *Why:* research — the Australian captain sits on the
panel and advocates; it's how "why isn't X picked" stories happen. *What:* `SquadAnnouncement`
carries a `SelectionRationale` (per debated slot) surfaced as news; a politicised panel's
rationale is visibly thin. *Where:* `SelectionPanelService`, `NewsEngine`. *Effort:* **S-M**.

**21. "Next in line" pipeline signal.** A player told by the selectors he's next in the queue for
a spot develops faster / handles the eventual debut better (and it's news). *Why:* research —
pre-series consultation where the captain "advocates for emerging talent"; it's how a real
pathway feels. *What:* `NationalPoolEntry` gains a `NextInLine` flag the panel sets; feeds
`MatchDevelopmentService` and the `PressureMoment` machinery. *Where:* `NationalPoolService`,
`SelectionPanelService`. *Effort:* **S**.

**22. Format-split workload contracts.** A player rested from a bilateral ODI series to be fresh
for a Test tour / a franchise final. *Why:* research — "the India squad is selected solely on
workload management"; we have `WorkloadRotationService` but it's per-fixture, not
cross-series/cross-format. *What:* `WorkloadRotationService` reads the *next 3 months* of fixtures
across formats + franchise commitments and produces a rest plan the panel applies. *Where:*
`WorkloadRotationService`, `NationalSelectionService`. *Effort:* **M**.

### C4 — Franchise ↔ international ↔ domestic integration, coaching worldwide

**23. Fixture-clash cancellation.** A franchise window that overlaps a bilateral series forces
the weaker board to cancel/shorten the series (England's 2024 SA tour). *Why:* it's the single
most-cited real consequence of franchise growth and it's a genuine strategic pressure on the
calendar. *What:* `CompetitionSeasonRunner` / the fixture layer detects an unavoidable clash
between an `International` window and a franchise window for a shared player pool; the board with
less clout cancels, taking a reputation + finance hit, and it's a big news story. *Where:*
`CompetitionSeasonRunner`, `WorldClockService.ProcessCompetitionWindows`. *Effort:* **M**.

**24. A coach's global CV / circuit reputation.** Voges (Perth), McCullum (KKR→England),
Flower/Moody run the franchise circuit; a coach's *worldwide* record (titles across leagues, not
just at one club) is what gets him the next job and drives his fee. *Why:* we have
`CoachCareerRecord` but it's club-scoped; the franchise-circuit coach is a real archetype we don't
capture. *What:* `Coach.CircuitRecord` (franchise titles/finals across all leagues); it feeds
`FranchiseCoachService.FindAvailableCoach` (a proven franchise coach is hired first and paid
more) and `CoachJobMarketService` (a domestic club covets a hot franchise coach). *Where:*
`Coach`, `FranchiseCoachService`, `CoachCareerService`. *Effort:* **S-M**.

**25. Campaign fees + a worldwide earnings ledger.** A franchise coaching stint pays a **campaign
fee** (not a year's salary); a coach's total annual income = board contract + N franchise
campaigns; "hybrid income >$2m/year" is the real headline. *Why:* the user asked for this
explicitly; it makes the coach an economic actor with a career trajectory. *What:*
`Coach.CareerEarnings` split by `{Domestic, National, Franchise}`; `FranchiseCoachService`
pays a campaign fee scaled by the league's prestige and the coach's circuit record; a coach who
has a big franchise year becomes harder for his domestic club to keep. *Where:* `FranchiseCoachService`,
`CoachCareerService`, `SeasonFinanceService`. *Effort:* **S-M**.

**26. Assistant/specialist coaches also work the circuit.** Hussey (batting) + Fleming (head) as
a pair; specialists move between leagues too. *Why:* completes the picture and gives
`StaffMember` a franchise dimension. *What:* `FranchiseCoachService` also fills 2-3 specialist
seats for a campaign from off-contract staff, campaign fee each. *Where:* `FranchiseCoachService`.
*Effort:* **S**.

**27. A franchise-to-international talent pathway.** A player who lights up a franchise league
forces his way into his national pool (real: many T20I debuts come off an Emerging Player award).
*Why:* it closes the loop between the three worlds and it's a genuine modern route to the top.
*What:* `SelectionPanelService` / `NationalPoolService.ReviewPool` weights a big franchise season
(from `SeasonContributions`) toward a watchlist call-up. *Where:* `NationalPoolService`. *Effort:*
**S** (mostly a new input to an existing review).

### C5 — Media, news, narrative, broadcast

**28. A per-nation media-market model.** Indian press ≠ Australian press ≠ English press
(intensity, home bias, tabloid vs broadsheet, how fast they turn on a captain). *Why:* research —
national press rivalries and the "obliged to boost the incumbent" dynamic are real culture; right
now our news is nationless. *What:* a `MediaMarket` per nation (Intensity, HomeBias, Volatility);
it scales `NewsEngine` prominence for that nation's teams/players, how sharply `PressConferenceService`
storylines escalate, and how quickly a losing run becomes a "sack him" story. *Where:*
`NewsEngine`, `PressConferenceService`, `PunditService`. *Effort:* **M**.

**29. A season/tournament narrative tracker.** Storylines that *build and pay off*: "breakout star"
(a young player on a run → gets a POTM → gets picked for his country), "captain under fire" (a
losing run → hostile pressers → board statement → sack or reprieve), "the flop" (a marquee
auction buy failing → pundit pile-on → dropped). *Why:* the user asked for narrative; it's the
difference between "a list of events" and "a season that feels like it had a story". *What:* a
`NarrativeService` that opens/advances/closes storyline objects from the event stream and emits
richer, connected news (and feeds `PressureMoment` / `BoardConfidence`). *Where:* new service on
the weekly/monthly tick, reads `NewsArchive`. *Effort:* **M-L**.

**30. Live leaderboards as news (Orange/Purple Cap, MVP).** Per competition: most runs, most
wickets, most sixes, a computed MVP; published weekly and at season end with a `TournamentAward`.
*Why:* research — these are core to how a tournament is followed and awarded; we only do
player-of-series + emerging. *What:* `RankingService`-style running tallies per active season from
`SeasonContributions` + `CareerStats` deltas; weekly digest lines + end-of-season awards +
morale/reputation bumps. *Where:* `CompetitionSeasonRunner`, `AwardsService`, `NewsEngine`.
*Effort:* **S-M**.

**31. Team of the tournament.** Real award; a computed best XI of the competition, published at
the awards ceremony. *Why:* cheap, and a nice bit of recognition/reputation. *What:* end-of-season
`XiSelectionService`-style pick over `SeasonContributions`; `TournamentAward` events, one per
selected player. *Where:* `CompetitionSeasonRunner.FinishSeason`. *Effort:* **S**.

**32. Broadcast-rights layer.** A competition sells its rights for a multi-year deal; the deal
size scales with `Competition.Reputation` + participant market size and feeds the franchise
central pool and domestic broadcast money (both already exist — this makes them a *deal* that's
negotiated and renewed, with news). *Why:* it's the actual engine of the franchise economy and
it's a natural extension of `BroadcastRevenueService`. *What:* a `BroadcastDeal` on `Competition`
(value, years-remaining, holder); renewed periodically with a news event; a competition whose
standing has risen lands a bigger deal. *Where:* `BroadcastRevenueService`, `CompetitionReputationService`.
*Effort:* **M**.

**33. Pundit conflicts of interest.** An ex-player pundit is softer on his old club / his old
nation, harder on a rival. *Why:* research — "obliged to boost struggling incumbents… praise
their boards"; it's a characterful detail that makes punditry feel real rather than neutral.
*What:* `PunditService` opinions carry a `formerAffiliation`; the take is spun toward it. *Where:*
`PunditService`. *Effort:* **S**.

**34. Auction-specific media beyond preview/report.** We added preview + report + press
conferences (follow-up). Extend: a *mock auction* / predictions piece a week before ("who will
RCB target"), a *winners and losers* verdict two days after that other franchises' fans react to
(fan sentiment), and a *player reaction* line (a big-money buy / an unsold star). *Why:* the user
asked for "everything in detail" around auction media; this is the depth. *What:*
`FranchiseAuctionMediaService` gains `MockAuction` (pre) and `WinnersAndLosers` (post) passes.
*Where:* `FranchiseAuctionMediaService`, wired in `RunFranchiseAuctionIfNeeded`. *Effort:* **S**.

### C6 — Cross-cutting / innovation

**35. A unified `TacticalPlan` for the AI, per format.** Today the AI passes `null` and the match
engine derives everything. Give each AI side a real `TacticalPlan` built from: its analyst report
(C1), its captain's `CaptaincyProfile`, the opposition's known weaknesses, the conditions, and
the match situation — and let a *poor* AI build a *bad* plan. *Why:* it makes AI-vs-AI matches
tactically legible (a post-match report could say "India got their plans wrong against the left-
handers") and it's the foundation C1/C2/C29 all lean on. *Where:* a new `AiTacticalPlanner` called
by `FixturePlayService`. *Effort:* **M**.

**36. Conditions-and-opposition XI selection (the deferred half of "AI depth").** CLAUDE.md's
Slice 6d/6f list it: pick the XI for *this* pitch and *this* opponent (an extra spinner on a
turner, a left-arm option against a right-heavy top order, drop a bunny). We have the pieces
(`XiSelectionService` conditions tilt, `PlanFitService`) — make it a real per-fixture decision.
*Why:* it's the last big "AI manager" gap and it's what separates a good AI coach from a
roster-filler. *Where:* `XiSelectionService` + `FixturePlayService`. *Effort:* **M**.

**37. A "player role clarity" concept.** A player who knows his role (finisher, enforcer,
powerplay bowler) performs closer to his ceiling; a player shuffled around underperforms. *Why:*
it's a real modern-cricket idea ("we've given him clarity on his role") and it ties selection,
captaincy and development together. *What:* `Player.AssignedRole` set by the coach; a match in
his assigned role gets a small confidence/performance bonus, out of it a penalty (we have
`SituationalPerformanceModifier` for the natural-role version — this is the *coach-assigned*
version). *Where:* `SituationalPerformanceModifier`, `TacticalPlan`. *Effort:* **S-M**.

**38. Multi-year franchise dynasty tracking.** A franchise that keeps a core (reserve list + RTM +
smart retention) over 3-4 years builds a dynasty (CSK); one that rebuilds every mega auction
churns. *Why:* it rewards the long-game archetype and gives the franchise world a memory. *What:*
`Team.DynastyRating` from squad continuity + trophies; feeds fan sentiment, sponsor value, and a
"culture" bonus (small `PerformanceMultiplier`). *Where:* `FranchiseFinanceService` /
a new annual pass. *Effort:* **S-M**.
